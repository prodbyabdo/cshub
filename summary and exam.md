To prepare for a multiple-choice question (MCQ) exam in accounting, you should focus on mastering fundamental principles, understanding the structure of financial statements, and practicing specific exam techniques.
Core Accounting Principles and Definitions
The Accounting Equation: This is the foundation of all financial accounting
.
Assets = Capital + Liabilities
.
Alternatively, Capital = Assets - Liabilities
.
Double-Entry Rules: Every transaction affects at least two items
.
Assets: Increase (Debit), Decrease (Credit)
.
Liabilities & Capital: Increase (Credit), Decrease (Debit)
.
Expenses: Always entered as a Debit
.
Revenue: Always entered as a Credit
.
Key Concepts:
Prudence: Ensure profits are not overstated and assets are not overvalued
.
Going Concern: Assume the business will continue for the foreseeable future
.
Accruals (Matching): Profit is the difference between revenue and the expenses incurred to generate it, regardless of when cash is paid
.
Materiality: Only include information significant enough to interest stakeholders
.
Essential Formulas and Ratios
MCQ exams often test your ability to calculate missing figures using these relationships:
Profitability:
Gross Profit: Sales Revenue - Cost of Goods Sold
.
Net Profit: Gross Profit + Other Income - Other Expenses
.
Mark-up vs. Margin: Mark-up is profit as a percentage of cost price, while margin is profit as a percentage of selling price
.
Liquidity:
Current Ratio: Current Assets ÷ Current Liabilities
.
Acid Test Ratio: (Current Assets - Stock) ÷ Current Liabilities
.
Efficiency:
Stock Turnover: Cost of Goods Sold ÷ Average Stock
.
Financial Statements Components
Trading Account: Used to calculate Gross Profit
.
Profit and Loss Account: Used to calculate Net Profit
.
Balance Sheet: A "position statement" showing assets, liabilities, and capital at a specific point in time
.
Fixed Assets: Long-term use, not for resale (e.g., machinery, vans)
.
Current Assets: Short-term, liquid (e.g., stock, debtors, cash)
.
Current Liabilities: Debts due within one year
.
MCQ Exam Techniques
Read the Rubric: Follow all instructions exactly (e.g., how many questions to answer)
.
Time Management: Calculate the time allowed per mark. For a 3-hour (180 min) 100-mark exam, spend roughly 1.8 minutes per mark
.
Tackle Easy Questions First: Build confidence and secure "warm-up" marks before moving to difficult ones
.
Eliminate Distractors: MCQs typically provide one correct "key" and several "distractors" (incorrect options). Use logic to rule out obviously wrong answers
.
Guess if Necessary: Unless there is a penalty for wrong answers, guess if you are unsure; you may be right by chance
.
Don't Over-Analyse: For items like a Balance Sheet that doesn't balance, it is often better to move on and secure marks on other questions rather than wasting time hunting for a single-mark error
.

To follow the textbook format for balancing off accounts, you must adhere to a specific sequence of five stages to ensure your T-accounts are professional and accurate
. First, add up both sides of the account to find their respective totals without writing anything in the account yet
. Deduct the smaller total from the larger total to find the difference, which is your balance
.
Enter the balance on the side with the smallest total and describe it as "Balance c/d" (carried down)
. Use the last day of the period for this entry
. Draw a single line above the totals and a double line underneath them, ensuring the totals on both sides are on the exact same horizontal level
. Finally, enter the same balance figure on the line below the double lines on the opposite side of the account
. Describe this as "Balance b/d" (brought down) and use the first day of the next period as the date
.
