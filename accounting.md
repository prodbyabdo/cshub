# File 2: The MCQ Cheatcode (Chapters 1-6)

### 1. The Golden Rule (Accounting Equation)

* 
**Assets - Liabilities = Capital** 


* Assets: What the business *owns* (Van, Building, Inventory, Cash, Debtors). 


* Liabilities: What the business *owes* (Loans, Creditors, Overdraft). 


* Capital: The owner's investment (The business owes this to the owner). 



### 2. The "DEAD CLIC" Mnemonic

To know whether to **Debit (Dr)** or **Credit (Cr)** an account:

* **D**ebit: **E**xpenses, **A**ssets, **D**rawings (Increase these with Dr).
* **C**redit: **L**iabilities, **I**ncome (Revenue), **C**apital (Increase these with Cr).

### 3. Key Transaction Logics

* 
**Buying on Credit:** Dr Asset/Purchase, Cr Creditor (Account Payable). 


* 
**Selling on Credit:** Dr Debtor (Account Receivable), Cr Sales. 


* 
**Returns Inwards:** Goods coming back from customers (Dr Returns Inwards, Cr Debtor). 


* 
**Returns Outwards:** Goods we send back to suppliers (Dr Creditor, Cr Returns Outwards). 



### 4. Balancing Accounts (The c/d and b/d)

* 
**Balance c/d (carried down):** The amount needed to make both sides equal at the end of the month. 


* 
**Balance b/d (brought down):** The *real* balance of the account at the start of the next month. 


* If b/d is on the **Debit** side, it's a **Debit Balance** (Asset/Expense). 


* If b/d is on the **Credit** side, it's a **Credit Balance** (Liability/Income). 



### 5. Trial Balance Essentials

* 
**Purpose:** To check if total Debits = total Credits. 


* 
**It does NOT find all errors:** It won't find "Errors of Omission" (forgetting a transaction entirely) or "Errors of Commission" (putting a Dr in the wrong person's account). 



### 6. Definitions for CS Students

* 
**Inventory:** Goods held for the purpose of reselling. 


* 
**Non-current Assets:** Long-term items (Fixed Assets) used to run the business, not for resale. 


* 
**Accounts Receivable (Debtors):** People who owe the business money. 


* 
**Accounts Payable (Creditors):** People the business owes money to.
